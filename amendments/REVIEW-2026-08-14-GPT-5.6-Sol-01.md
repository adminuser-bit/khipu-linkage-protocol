GPT-5.6 Sol — August 14, 2026.

1. FATAL — A20/A21; finite N1 support is incompatible with the new Holm denominator.
Failure scenario: A21 says an N1 null is scoreable whenever it has at least 1,000 distinct admissible permutation images. That criterion was adequate only for the uncorrected frozen requirement (p<0.001). A20 now correctly fixes (m) over the full search and therefore imposes the first Holm threshold (0.001/m). At the order-of-magnitude universe specified here, (m=619\times4\times100=247{,}600), so the first threshold is approximately 4.04×10⁻⁹. An exact randomization test with only 1,000 distinct states has minimum attainable (p\approx10^{-3}); even a million states bottoms out around (10^{-6}). No amount of Monte Carlo resampling can create probability mass below the finite exact permutation support. A20's requested (N\ge10m/0.001) equals 2.476 billion draws per null per survivor, which gives Monte Carlo resolution ≈4.04×10⁻¹⁰, but only if the underlying null has sufficient support. A21's 1,000-state rule therefore conflicts directly with A20.
Required fix: Replace A21 with:
"For every triple, let (M_1) be the number of distinct admissible N1 permutation images. A triple is confirmatorily scoreable only if the frozen Stage-2 procedure has minimum attainable (p < 0.001/m). Under full exact enumeration this requires (1/M_1 < 0.001/m); using the conservative rank-1 Holm floor, (M_1>1000m). Monte Carlo draws never increase effective support beyond the exact permutation universe. Triples failing this condition are unscoreable and remain in the frozen multiplicity denominator with no positive-claim eligibility."
For (m\approx247{,}600), that conservative support floor is >247.6 million distinct N1 states, not 1,000.
2. FATAL — A20/A22; N2 still obtains arbitrarily fine p-values by pseudoreplicating a small number of decoy documents.
Failure scenario: The red team correctly objected that the original N2 was 100 document identities padded by 9,900 permutations, so 99% of its nominal sample size was not independent decoy-document evidence. A22 adds a rank-1-among-100-real-decoys requirement but leaves the "permutation-padded N2 p-value" in the adjudication path. A20 then permits billions of N2 "draws." This means the numerical p-resolution can become arbitrarily fine while the actual document-identity support remains ~100. A candidate can therefore satisfy a 10⁻⁹-scale Holm threshold by repeatedly permuting the same hundred documents. That does not faithfully implement the frozen null, which is explicitly the score against other archival documents of the same genre and region.
Required fix: Do not pool permutations as if they were additional document identities. Define N2 now as a clustered mixture: each matched decoy document receives exactly equal weight; within-document permutations estimate that document's conditional tail, and the frozen N2 statistic combines those per-document tails by a specified formula without treating within-document draws as independent documents. Freeze the combination rule, tie rule, and finite-support attainability calculation in A20 itself. The rank-1 identity condition remains conjunctive. If no statistically defensible N2 construction can reach (0.001/m) without pseudo-replication, then the current frozen H-KHI-1 architecture remains unpassable and must be amended rather than numerically padded.
3. MAJOR — A20; Stage 2 is valid only if its null simulation is independent of Stage 1.
Failure scenario: Stage 1 selects triples because their first 9,999/10,000 simulated draws happened to look unusually favorable. If Stage 2 merely continues those same permutation streams and retains the favorable Stage-1 draws in the final p-value, selection on Monte Carlo noise makes the confirmatory p-value anti-conservative. A20 currently says survivors are "re-scored" but does not explicitly forbid reuse of Stage-1 null draws.
Required fix: Add: "Stage-2 simulation uses a new independently seeded random stream. No Stage-1 null draw, exceedance count, adaptive proposal, or fitted tail parameter may enter the Stage-2 p-value. Stage-1 results are discarded for inference. A sequential/continued-sampling design is prohibited unless a separately frozen always-valid sequential Monte Carlo test is substituted before scoring."
4. MAJOR — A20/A17; the exact-tail/importance-sampling escape hatch is itself unfrozen discretion.
Failure scenario: A20 allows either billions of ordinary draws or "an exact/importance-sampled tail computation whose method, estimator, and conservative-bound construction are frozen in the A0 appendix." Those alternatives can give materially different answers at 10⁻⁹-scale tails. Choosing the estimator after seeing permutation-space sizes, tie structure, or document distributions is precisely the sort of underpowered/fallback discretion the red team was asked to remove. It also conflicts with A17, which says all operational appendices must be complete before Run 3 designs contact data.
Required fix: Freeze one Stage-2 inferential algorithm in this amendment package. If importance sampling is retained, specify proposal distribution, stopping rule, seed construction, estimator, confidence-bound formula, minimum effective sample size, diagnostics, treatment of zero exceedances, and the rule that failure of diagnostics makes the triple unscoreable. Do not defer choice of the tail method to A0.
5. MAJOR — A20; the definition of (E) incorrectly converts a pair-level power floor into a khipu-level eligibility decision.
Failure scenario: A20 defines `U1_eligible` as a subset of khipus having "≥20 alignable entries," then crosses that set with all four groupings and all documents. The frozen design, however, states that H-KHI-1 pairs with <20 alignable entries are not scoreable; grouping changes the number of khipu groups, and document length changes alignment opportunity. "Alignable entries" is therefore not obviously a property of a khipu alone. A motivated operator could define khipu eligibility using whichever grouping yields the convenient result.
Required fix: Define eligibility at the triple level using quantities available without computing the linkage score:
(E={(k,g,d): k,g,d\text{ satisfy a fully specified pre-scoreability predicate}}).
Define every component of that predicate now. Alternatively put every otherwise eligible triple in (E), freeze (m), and assign unscoreable triples no positive eligibility without removing them from (m).
6. MAJOR — A20/A21/A22; post-freeze "unscoreable" findings must never shrink the Holm denominator.
Failure scenario: A20 says every element of (E) is scored and (m=|E|); A21 and A22 can subsequently declare pairs unscoreable because of finite N1 support or inadequate decoys. If those pairs are removed from (m), the operator can obtain a less stringent Holm threshold after inspecting data structure.
Required fix: State explicitly: "Once `freeze/A0-universe-v1` fixes (m), no later scoreability failure reduces (m). An unscoreable triple remains a registered test with no positive eligibility and is assigned the conservative equivalent of p=1 for multiplicity accounting."
7. MAJOR — A20; the subsampling option leaves the actual research universe discretionary.
Failure scenario: "If compute requires subsampling" permits a deterministic subsample, with (m) then defined as that smaller subset. Subsampling is statistically defensible if genuinely ex ante, but the phrase "if compute requires" allows the choice to be made after learning the archive-universe size and possibly its metadata. It also invalidates the design's registered null interpretation as an "upper bound … over an enumerated search universe" if only a subset is searched.
Required fix: Either choose full cross-product now, or freeze before archive enumeration a mechanical compute-budget rule such as `if |E_raw| > M_max, sample exactly M_max triples using SHA256-ordering with seed X`. Any null-result language must then say "over the deterministic sampled universe," not the full enumerated archive universe.
8. MAJOR — A22; decoy construction retains several result-moving degrees of freedom.
Failure scenario: "entry-count stratum" has no bin boundaries; exact treatment of mixed document types/regions is unstated; decoy membership is manually frozen rather than generated by a frozen selection algorithm; and a deficient stratum may later be "extended by a dated … addendum." An operator can select easy decoys or redefine/extend a sparse stratum after seeing which candidates need help.
Required fix: Before any numeric document content is exposed, freeze: exact entry-count bins; metadata normalization; matching hierarchy; tie handling; and a deterministic decoy-selection algorithm from the eligible-document universe. After `A0-universe-v1`, no same-run extension is permitted. A deficient stratum makes its candidates unscoreable for that run; it may be fixed only in a new preregistered run.
9. MAJOR — A23; the positive-control grouping is internally contradictory.
Failure scenario: A23 freezes the Santa Valley test under G-2, color-band grouping. The frozen Run-3 design defines G-2 as maximal same-color runs and separately defines G-4 as fixed-width six-cord grouping, "the Santa Valley convention." These are not the same algorithm. The quarantine trigger is therefore still ambiguous.
Required fix: Define a unique positive-control-only grouping algorithm `PC-G` reproducing exactly the published Medrano–Urton grouping procedure, with code/test fixture frozen before execution. It has zero evidential weight and remains outside the Holm family. Do not call it G-2 or G-4 unless it is literally identical to that registered grouping.
10. FATAL — A25; the amendment openly widens a frozen requirement.
Failure scenario: The canonical design requires "independent double keying … by two human transcribers." A25 explicitly replaces that with two AI systems and calls the substitute "a deviation weaker than the frozen requirement." Disclosure does not cure a prohibited widening. Under the stated rules, this amendment cannot be frozen as written.
Required fix: Preserve the two-human requirement. If two humans cannot be obtained, restrict H-KHI-1 execution to documents whose relevant numeric/group-structure transcription was already produced under acceptable scholarly human editorial control, and block the unpublished-manuscript/H-KHI-2 E3 arm until the registered human-QC requirement can be satisfied. A weaker substitution may be reported as a pilot, but its outputs cannot enter the preregistered adjudication.
11. MAJOR — A25; cross-lineage AI agreement is correlated with common published training data and is not independent transcription QC.
Failure scenario: Both keyers may have memorized the same published visita edition. Different model lineages do not make the errors independent if both learned the same edition, secondary transcription, or famous Santa Valley structure. In the positive control, a model that recognizes the document could normalize ambiguous grouping or numbers toward the known correspondence. Frozen non-LLM scoring does not cure this because the corrupted transcript is the scoring input. A25's 5% human sample is the only genuinely source-grounded check.
Required fix if AI keying is retained for non-adjudicative preprocessing: keyers receive only anonymized page/crop images, with title/archive/document identity removed; network and retrieval disabled; exact model/version/settings frozen before the first page; all outputs automatically archived. Agreement is described only as inter-model agreement, never independent accuracy evidence.
12. MAJOR — A25; the human spot-check rule is both underpowered and sometimes impossible.
Failure scenario: A25 requires "≥5% … minimum 200 fields per document," while document eligibility begins at ≥20 numeric entries. A 50-field document cannot yield 200 distinct spot-checks. More importantly, 200 error-free checks do not establish an error rate ≤0.5% with strong confidence: with zero observed errors, roughly 598 independent checks are required for a one-sided 95% binomial upper bound below 0.5%.
Required fix: If this check survives as an auxiliary QC measure, specify `n_check = min(F, max(ceil(0.05F), 598))`, where (F) is the number of independently checkable fields; if all fields are checked, report the exact census error rate. Pass only if the one-sided 95% exact binomial upper confidence bound is ≤0.005. Group boundaries/hierarchy should be checked separately and require 100% agreement or explicit "uncertain/unscoreable" coding.
13. MAJOR — A25; disagreement adjudication remains operator-discretionary.
Failure scenario: "disagreements resolved by a third independent key plus operator adjudication" allows the operator to choose among three conflicting readings. A single changed number or group boundary can alter an exact-match score.
Required fix: Freeze deterministic resolution: two-of-three identical keys determine the value; three-way disagreement or structural disagreement is sent to the independent human custodian/paleographer blind to all linkage scores. If no unambiguous source reading results, code the field missing/uncertain under a frozen rule; the operator never chooses a value.
14. FATAL — A24/A25/A12; "operator inevitably has global visibility" breaks the H-KHI-2 E3 seal.
Failure scenario: A12's entire E3 instrument depends on attributes/names/status being sealed with an independent custodian until Team P deposits predictions. The design likewise says the custodian receives the full transcript and Teams L/P do not. A24 nevertheless declares that the operator "inevitably has global visibility," and A25 requires that same operator to inspect source pages during human QC. Source pages can contain the very names/status/moiety attributes that Phase C is supposed to seal. Once the operator has seen them, the operator can leak them consciously or unconsciously through Team-P prompts, taxonomy choices, code, or troubleshooting.
Required fix: Replace "global visibility" with an explicit exception: the operator is cryptographically and procedurally denied all Phase-C sealed attribute content until Team-P prediction deposit. Full-document QC for any potentially E3-eligible document must be performed by the independent custodian/other authorized human; the operator may see only redacted numeric-field crops. Custodian logs and encrypted sealed files must not be operator-readable before deposit.
15. MAJOR — A24; prompt commitment does not prevent silent agent reruns or output cherry-picking.
Failure scenario: A24 commits prompts/configurations "no later than invocation" but does not require every invocation and every output—including aborted, failed, or inconvenient attempts—to be automatically committed. A motivated operator can run the same keyer/triage agent repeatedly and retain the preferred output.
Required fix: All AI invocations occur through a frozen wrapper that: assigns a monotonic run ID; commits prompt hash/model/version/decoding settings before invocation; automatically hashes and appends the complete raw output and error status immediately afterward; forbids silent retries; and counts every retry capable of affecting search/transcription as a registered attempt. Manual direct invocations are inadmissible.
16. MAJOR — A24/A25; exact model identities and decoding configurations are not frozen.
Failure scenario: A25 requires merely "different model lineages" and logs the tool/version when used. The operator could try several models or temperatures after seeing transcription difficulty and select the pair that agrees most often.
Required fix: Before the first document is keyed, freeze exact model IDs/versions, API mode, system prompt, temperature/top-p, seed where supported, vision resolution/cropping rules, permitted tools, network state, and fallback hierarchy. Failure/unavailability of a frozen model triggers a new amendment, not a quiet substitution.
17. FATAL — A26; retroactive "ratification" is a prohibited widening of A17.
Failure scenario: A17 says that all pre-scoring appendix items must be completed before Run-3 experiment designs contact data, and that an incomplete appendix at data contact is a protocol violation. The red team explicitly noted that its invented "audit-grade" carve-out does not appear in A17. A26 now declares selected real-data acts not to count as data contact and "ratifies" prior acts retroactively. That changes the meaning of an already-binding requirement after the prohibited event occurred. It is exactly a retrospective widening.
Required fix: Delete the retroactive ratification. Replace A26 with two parts:
(a) Historical declaration: enumerate every pre-appendix data act already performed and record it as an A17 protocol deviation; do not erase it. State exactly what values/statistics were exposed and which later design choices could have been informed by them.
(b) Prospective acquisition rule for future work: after a design-rule freeze but before inferential scoring, a custodian may perform only enumerated byte/schema acts—download, cryptographic hash, file size, schema/table counts, mechanically specified identifier resolution. These acts may populate manifests but may not change thresholds, groupings, eligibility rules, nulls, candidate selection, power floors, or scoring code. Content-level statistics and feature distributions are excluded. This prospective rule requires its own explicit amendment; it cannot rewrite the history of A17.
18. MAJOR — A27; the canonical record cannot be "the default branch."
Failure scenario: A27 identifies the mutable default branch as the canonical tribunal record and incorporates `GOVERNANCE.md` R1–R5 by reference. The five canonical files supplied for this review do not contain GOVERNANCE.md, so I cannot verify branch-protection, anchoring, rewrite, or timestamp semantics. More fundamentally, a moving branch name is not an immutable scientific record.
Required fix: The freeze state must be a signed tag pointing to an exact full commit hash, externally anchored before any gated action. The draft should name that commit/tag explicitly. Subsequent execution logs must form append-only descendants of that commit; no history rewrite/force push; each gated event cites the preceding anchored commit. Include the canonical bytes/hash of GOVERNANCE.md in this amendment package or inline R1–R5. A27 is not reviewable to acceptance until that is supplied.
19. MAJOR — A20/A24; D_cand is not mechanically defined, leaving the largest search-selection lever open.
Failure scenario: A20 treats `D_cand` as "the frozen candidate-document list" but never states how it is derived. The design previously allowed archival/LLM triage and described a list of archive leads. If an LLM or operator chooses which apparently eligible documents become candidates before A0, memorized literature or subjective plausibility can enrich the search set for likely matches while excluding hard negatives. Freezing a biased list afterward does not remove the bias.
Required fix: Define `D_cand` as all documents meeting the frozen metadata-only eligibility rule in the enumerated series, minus a decoy panel selected by a deterministic precommitted algorithm and the positive-control document. LLM triage may reorder processing only after membership is mechanically fixed; it cannot determine membership.
20. MAJOR — A22; decoy-panel membership itself must be randomly/deterministically assigned, not curated.
Failure scenario: The operator can put awkward documents into the candidate set and easy documents into the decoy set, or vice versa, while satisfying every stated stratum rule. A22 freezes membership but not the selection mechanism.
Required fix: Within each frozen metadata stratum, assign eligible documents to candidate vs decoy status by a deterministic hash lottery with a published seed before numeric transcription. The positive-control document is the only predesignated exception. Deposit the complete assignment.
21. MAJOR — H-KHI-2/G5 omission; multiplicity across new pairs and attribute endpoints is unspecified.
Failure scenario: Global G5 requires Holm correction over all registered tests actually run. H-KHI-2 permits "at least one" new pair and the design contains at least two attribute families—attachment→status/moiety and color→name-class. If three new pairs pass H-KHI-1, the operator could test six pair×endpoint combinations and report whichever crosses p<0.01. No H-KHI-2 multiplicity rule is currently frozen.
Required fix: Define the H-KHI-2 family as every `(new_pair, attribute_endpoint)` test actually run and Holm-correct at family α=0.01; alternatively choose exactly one target pair and one endpoint by a deterministic rule fixed before Phase B. All failures remain logged.
22. MAJOR — H-KHI-2/A24 omission; target-pair selection can be outcome-informed.
Failure scenario: Several H-KHI-1 pairs may pass. A single operator who has seen transcription material can choose which one advances to Phase C, potentially knowing which document contains especially clean or favorable attributes. The current protocol says only "at least one."
Required fix: Either run Phase C on all qualifying new pairs, subject to Finding 21's Holm correction, or select the target by a pre-frozen deterministic hash order using only pair identifiers, with the operator and Team P blind to sealed attributes.
23. MAJOR — A25/H-KHI-2; published transcriptions are incompatible with the contamination-resistant E3 claim unless explicitly demoted.
Failure scenario: A25 makes published scholarly transcriptions the preferred search source. But the Run-3 design describes the E3 instrument as running on unpublished archival documents newly transcribed for this run, precisely because those attributes cannot be in model training data. A "new linkage" to a long-published census may be new to the project while its names/statuses are already memorized by Team-P models. Hashing the prediction before unmasking does not erase weight-level contamination.
Required fix: Add an E3 eligibility rule: the target document's sealed attribute content must not have been publicly transcribed, digitized, or published before the latest training cutoff of any Team-P pretrained component. If that cannot be established, H-KHI-2 may run only as an E2 retrospective/generalization test, not the E3 instrument.
24. MAJOR — H-KHI-2; taxonomy, prediction model, and released-fields baseline are frozen too late.
Failure scenario: The design says Team P freezes the name-class taxonomy "before deposit," which can occur after Team P has received the numeric fields of the target pair. A flexible taxonomy or baseline architecture can then be chosen to fit peculiarities of the selected target.
Required fix: Freeze the complete attribute taxonomy, mapping rules, model architecture/weights or fitting algorithm, feature set, and released-fields-only baseline before Team P receives any numeric fields from any prospective target pair. The only subsequent computation may be deterministic inference on the already-frozen model.
25. MAJOR — A21/A22/G9; "unscoreable" triples invalidate the registered family-null interpretation unless their mass is explicitly bounded.
Failure scenario: The design says no new match would yield a "multiplicity-honest upper bound on the khipu–archive linkage rate over an enumerated search universe." After A21/A22, however, potentially large portions of that universe may be unscoreable because their N1 support is too small or their decoy stratum is underfilled. A null produced by refusing to test difficult triples is not an upper bound on linkage across the universe.
Required fix: Report `|E|`, number scoreable, and number unscoreable by reason. Unless every triple is scoreable—or a formal bound accounts for the missing portion—the G9 conclusion is restricted to: "No linkage passed among the scoreable registered triples; X/Y triples were unscoreable." Prohibit "upper bound over the enumerated universe" language.
26. MAJOR — A25; edition-selection and uncertainty conventions are unspecified.
Failure scenario: A25 says a published scholarly transcription becomes the primary key when available, but gives no rule if multiple editions disagree or if an edition contains supplied/restored numerals, uncertain digits, bracketed text, damaged lines, editorial subtotals, or reconstructed group boundaries. Choosing the favorable edition or treating reconstructed numerals as observed can move exact-match counts.
Required fix: Freeze an edition hierarchy independent of scores and a field-state vocabulary such as `{observed, editorially-restored, uncertain, illegible, supplied subtotal}`. Primary scoring uses only the predesignated admissible states; all others are excluded or enter a separately registered sensitivity treatment. Conflicting editions trigger the frozen hierarchy, never operator choice.
27. MINOR — A20 screening threshold itself is coherent, conditional on Findings 1–4 being fixed.
Stage-1 `p≤0.01` on both nulls is a defensible computational screen. With (m\approx247{,}600), a genuinely Holm-significant raw p near (4×10^{-9}) will overwhelmingly survive a 9,999-draw 0.01 screen. The screen is not the arithmetic defect; reuse of its Monte Carlo draws and the finite-support problem are.
28. MINOR — A23's exclusion of the Santa Valley control from Holm is not itself a widening.
Because the positive control is expressly calibration-only, carries zero evidential weight for new claims, and can only quarantine results, applying its p<0.001 requirement uncorrected does not make a substantive linkage claim easier to pass. The unresolved G-2/G-4 definition in Finding 9 must nevertheless be fixed.
29. MINOR — A22's added pure-decoy rank-1 requirement is a genuine narrowing.
Requiring the candidate to beat every actual matched decoy document adds a constraint; it does not widen H-KHI-1. It does not, by itself, repair the N2 p-value problem in Finding 2.
30. MINOR — A27's repository substitution is operationally acceptable in principle, but not yet verifiable.
Replacing unspecified "tribunal record" deposits with an immutable, externally timestamped git record would not change any scientific threshold. But acceptance is conditional on the exact-commit and GOVERNANCE fixes in Finding 18.
