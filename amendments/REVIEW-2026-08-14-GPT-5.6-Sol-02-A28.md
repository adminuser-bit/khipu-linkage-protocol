## Reviewer bottom line (verbatim)

I'd incorporate it, subject to the corrections. I don't think the concept is
fundamentally broken.
The core idea is good: keep the brutal confirmatory threshold exactly where it
is, while preserving potentially interesting sub-threshold observations as
explicitly exploratory output. That's scientifically healthier than either
suppressing them or quietly loosening H-KHI-1.
The one part that was genuinely dangerous was the future-run provision. As
drafted, it effectively said: screen 250,000 pairs, keep the lucky ones, then
next time test only those and call the new test fresh. That is not fresh
evidence. Once you fix that—same-data follow-up retains the original
selection/multiplicity burden; genuinely new independent evidence can provide
fresh confirmation—the biggest back door disappears.
I'd make three conceptual changes and then be comfortable freezing it:

* Rename "registered leads" to something deliberately sterile like
"screen-positive non-adjudicated triples." "Lead" will inevitably become
"promising lead" in ordinary speech.
* Require the report to put N screen-positives beside the expected null count,
not merely say "we found N." At ~247,600 tests, even under the overly
favorable independence assumption you'd expect about 25 double-p≤.01 hits
purely by chance; because N1/N2 are correlated, the actual null expectation
needs simulation/calibration.
* Make the future-use rule explicit: these triples can direct future research,
but they cannot be promoted to confirmation by simply re-running the same
khipu/document data. Confirmation requires independent held-out/new evidence,
or continuation under the original multiplicity family plus any additional
method-search penalty.

With those corrections, A28 serves a legitimate purpose: it gives you a
rigorous home for "interesting enough to investigate further" without
pretending that "interesting" means evidentially established.
So my tribunal recommendation would be: REVISE AND ADOPT, not abandon. I would
not freeze the verbatim draft, but I would absolutely keep the amendment.

## Full findings (verbatim)

GPT-5.6 Sol — August 14, 2026.

1. FATAL — A28.4 / G5; re-scoring a lead-selected pair on the same underlying data does not erase the original selection.
Failure scenario: Clause 4 says a lead may become the priority input to a future run and need only be "re-scored from scratch under that run's frozen rules," with the earlier Stage-1 result assigned zero evidential weight. That is not sufficient. The pair itself was selected because its realized linkage statistic was unusually favorable among approximately (m) tested triples. Recomputing permutations, changing seeds, or even using a somewhat revised null on the same khipu and same document values does not create independent evidence; the favorable fixed-data coincidence that caused selection remains. A future run that tests only the selected leads and Holm-corrects over the much smaller lead set would convert this run's exploratory tail selection directly into a confirmatory advantage. G5 exists specifically to prevent that kind of multiplicity laundering.
Required fix: Replace clause 4 with:
Future-use rule. A registered lead may seed future archival research but may not obtain confirmatory evidential status merely by being re-scored on the same underlying khipu–document data. A future same-data analysis is admissible only as a continuation of this run's original multiplicity family: the original frozen (m) remains the minimum multiplicity denominator, the original selection event is disclosed, and no scoring/null change informed by the lead may reduce that burden. Alternatively, a lead may be tested confirmatorily on genuinely independent evidence that played no role in this run's screening—for example a preregistered sealed portion of the archival record, a newly obtained independent document, or newly recorded khipu evidence. Only such independent evidence may constitute a new confirmatory family with its own multiplicity correction. Mere re-randomization or re-permutation of the same observations is not independent evidence.
2. MAJOR — A28.1; "registered lead" currently includes triples that are incapable of adjudication.
Failure scenario: Clause 1 requires only Stage-1 (p\le0.01) on both nulls and failure to pass Stage 2. Under A20/A21/A22, some triples will be unscoreable at Stage 2 because N1 has insufficient finite support or the required matched-decoy stratum does not exist. A pair can therefore become a "lead" even though the protocol has already established that it cannot be subjected to the registered confirmatory test. That creates a perverse route whereby the least adjudicable cases enter the future priority list. My main review already found that A21's present 1,000-state scoreability rule is itself insufficient once Holm is applied.
Required fix: Amend clause 1:
A registered lead must be confirmatorily scoreable under every frozen Stage-2 requirement and must actually complete Stage 2. A Stage-1-positive triple later found unscoreable is reported separately as a `screen-positive / unscoreable` triple, licenses no lead status, and may not enter the A28 future-priority list.
3. MAJOR — A28.1/A20; Stage-1 parameters become scientifically consequential once they define a named reporting band and therefore must be frozen exactly, not merely approximately.
Failure scenario: Before A28, Stage 1 was computational triage whose p-values had no adjudicative meaning. A28 turns the Stage-1 boundary into the membership rule for a persistent, citable artifact. A20 currently fixes N1 at 9,999 draws but N2 at "≥10,000", leaving the exact N2 draw count open; it also does not yet freeze all seed/retry/Monte-Carlo details. Different N2 draw counts can move borderline (p\approx0.01) triples across the lead boundary. Once "number of registered leads" is reported, there is an incentive to choose screening implementation details that inflate or suppress that count.
Required fix: A28 must cross-reference a strengthened A20 and state:
Stage-1 lead membership is computed using exactly 9,999 N1 draws and exactly 9,999 or exactly 10,000 N2 draws [choose one now], a frozen empirical-p formula, frozen tie treatment, independently frozen seeds/streams, and no adaptive extension or rerun. Any different draw count, seed replacement, estimator, or retry creates a different exploratory analysis and cannot alter the registered-lead list.
Also freeze whether exact enumeration replaces Monte Carlo when available and, if so, how the (p\le0.01) lead boundary is calculated.
4. MAJOR — A28.5; the raw lead count has no interpretable null expectation unless N1/N2 dependence is explicitly calibrated.
Failure scenario: With approximately m=619×4×100=247,600 screened triples, a lead requires two Stage-1 events: p_N1≤.01, p_N2≤.01. Under independent valid uniform null p-values, the joint probability is 10^-4, giving E[L]=247,600×10^-4≈24.76 pure-noise screen positives.
But the two p-values are plainly not guaranteed independent: they use the same observed linkage score and related document/value structure. With perfectly positively dependent valid p-values, P(p1≤.01,p2≤.01)=.01 and the expectation is as high as 247,600×.01=2,476. With only marginal validity and arbitrary dependence, the Fréchet range for the joint probability is effectively 0 to 0.01. Thus "N registered leads" has no meaningful chance baseline by itself. Because N1/N2 will probably be positively correlated, 24.76 should not be treated as the operative expectation.
Required fix: Before freezing A28, register a joint-null calibration procedure that estimates, for each scoreable triple or appropriately frozen stratum, q_i=P_H0(p_N1≤.01, p_N2≤.01), E_0[L]=Σ_i q_i, while preserving the actual N1/N2 dependence structure. Deposit the procedure before scoring. Every lead-count report must state observed N, calibrated E_0[L], a simulation/uncertainty interval, and the dependence-robust reference bounds. If such calibration is infeasible, reports must at minimum give the independence reference (~24.8 at m=247,600) and the valid worst-case upper reference (~2,476), explicitly saying that neither is an adjudicative test.
5. MAJOR — A28.3/A28.5; clause 3 prohibits aggregate evidential interpretation, but clause 5 invites exactly that interpretation by juxtaposing "zero passes" with "N leads."
Failure scenario: The sentence "zero pairs passed adjudication, and N registered leads survived screening" will predictably be read as "nothing met the extreme bar, but we found N promising signals," especially if N is numerically large. Calling them "leads" reinforces that inference. Clause 3 formally says counts cannot be directional findings, but the standardized null sentence makes the count rhetorically load-bearing. This is a practical claim back door even without a technical threshold change.
Required fix: Rename the band "screen-positive non-adjudicated triples" rather than "registered leads." "Lead" is itself an evidential term. Replace clause 5 with:
Required family-result phrasing: "Zero of (m) registered triples satisfied H-KHI-1 adjudication. (N) triples met the preregistered computational screening rule (p_N1≤0.01) and (p_N2≤0.01); the screening count is exploratory, has no E-class licensing, and must be interpreted against its preregistered joint-null expectation (E_0[L]). Screen-positive triples are not evidence of linkage."
6. MAJOR — A28.3; current communication restrictions do not bind abstracts, press releases, grant applications, talks, or selective citation strongly enough.
Failure scenario: Clause 3 forbids words such as "match" and "candidate match" in "reports," but an operator can still write a grant proposal saying "we identified 37 registered leads," put the strongest three in a conference slide, issue a press release saying "new khipu leads discovered," or cite an individual pair as motivation. The underlying tribunal record would be technically compliant while the practical scientific claim escapes through another communication channel.
Required fix: Expand clause 3:
The no-claim restriction applies to all program-controlled communications, including papers, abstracts, titles, press releases, websites, grant applications, presentations, interviews, repository READMEs, social-media posts, and correspondence describing results. No individual screen-positive triple may be highlighted, ranked, named, illustrated, or discussed outside the complete standardized results table except solely to document a protocol anomaly. The terms "lead," "signal," "suggestive," "promising," "near match," "candidate," "correspondence," and equivalent evidential language are prohibited for these triples. Third-party characterizations are not controlled by the protocol, but program participants may not endorse them.
7. MAJOR — A28.2; mandatory exhaustive reporting still permits salience cherry-picking unless ordering and presentation are frozen.
Failure scenario: Publishing every screen-positive pair technically satisfies clause 2, while a paper can still feature the smallest-p pair first, give it a figure, and bury the other 400 in a CSV. That functions as selective reporting despite full enumeration.
Required fix: Require:
Screen-positive triples are presented only in one standardized machine-readable table and, if reproduced in prose/PDF, in a deterministic non-evidential order (e.g. SHA-256 identifier order), never sorted by score or p-value in the primary report. No individual pair receives a figure, case study, narrative paragraph, or headline treatment. A separately downloadable p-sorted table may be generated mechanically only if clearly labeled exploratory and accompanied by the complete table and null-expectation information.
8. MAJOR — A28.4; "priority input for archive enumeration" can itself contaminate the next archive universe even if the same pair is never re-tested.
Failure scenario: Suppose a screen-positive pair involves khipu K and document D from region R. In a future run the team prioritizes archives around R, related communities, adjacent years, or the same ayllu because A28 told them where to look. Those newly discovered documents were not literally part of the original screen, but the search design was conditioned on the original noise realization. If the future run treats the resulting targeted archive universe as though it had been selected independently, the original screening statistic has indirectly shaped the confirmatory evidence.
Required fix: Distinguish exploratory discovery from confirmation:
Archive expansion motivated by screen-positive triples is explicitly lead-conditioned discovery. Documents discovered through that path may generate hypotheses but are not automatically confirmatory evidence for the motivating khipu. For an E2 linkage claim arising from a lead-conditioned archive search, either (a) a completely independent confirmatory archive stratum not selected using A28 information must be frozen before inspection, or (b) the multiplicity correction must cover the full lead-conditioned search tree, including every lead used to choose archives and every document inspected as a consequence. The search tree and branching decisions are logged under G5.
9. MAJOR — A28.4; if same-data future continuation is allowed, merely "inheriting this run's m" is necessary but not always sufficient.
Failure scenario: A future team could keep the original (m) but change the matching score, grouping rule, decoy stratification, or null construction after knowing which pairs screened well. The method change is then optimized on the selected cases. Using denominator (m) alone does not account for the additional method-selection/restart multiplicity.
Required fix: Add:
A same-data continuation may inherit the original (m) only if the original scoring statistic, grouping definition, null constructions, and eligibility rules are unchanged. Any lead-informed change to those elements constitutes an additional searched analysis and must either be included in a frozen multiplicity family covering all alternative methods tried or be treated as exploratory only. A method designed after viewing the leads cannot confirm those leads on the same data.
10. MAJOR — A28.1/A28.2; Stage-2 outcome should be part of the recorded lead object, not merely the numeric p-values.
Failure scenario: "Does not satisfy adjudication" conflates several very different states: both raw Stage-2 p-values are ordinary; one is extreme and one is not; both are <0.001 but fail Holm; an exact-tail diagnostic fails; the triple is unscoreable; or a decoy rank condition fails. These have different methodological meanings. If only p-values and "scoreability status" are reported, downstream users can quietly focus on the most favorable failure type.
Required fix: Define a closed reason code and require exactly one terminal status:
   * `S1_ONLY_FAIL_RAW`
   * `S2_FAIL_N1`
   * `S2_FAIL_N2`
   * `S2_FAIL_HOLM`
   * `S2_FAIL_DECOY_RANK`
   * `UNSCOREABLE_N1_SUPPORT`
   * `UNSCOREABLE_DECOY_STRATUM`
   * `PASS_ADJUDICATION`
Only completed, scoreable Stage-2 failures may enter the screen-positive future-priority artifact after Finding 2 is implemented.
11. MAJOR — A28.5; A28 must not alter the registered "multiplicity-honest upper bound" interpretation, and its current wording risks doing so.
Failure scenario: The Run-3 design currently pre-registers a null interpretation as a "multiplicity-honest upper bound on the khipu–archive linkage rate over an enumerated search universe." My main review already found that this statement is too broad if a material share of (E) is unscoreable. A28 creates another temptation: to say "the upper bound is zero adjudicated matches, although N leads remain." That turns a binary family-null into a quasi-estimate with a soft residual category that has no calibrated relationship to true linkage prevalence.
Required fix: A28 must state:
The screen-positive count does not modify, relax, interpolate, or qualify the H-KHI-1 adjudication threshold and is not an estimator or bound on the prevalence of true linkages. The registered family-null interpretation is based solely on adjudicated, scoreable tests. Any "upper bound" language must separately report the number and fraction of (E) that were confirmatorily scoreable and comply with the scoreability limitation registered elsewhere.
12. MAJOR — A28.6; prohibiting prose about near-threshold Stage-2 results is useful but insufficient if numeric precision itself becomes a ranking device.
Failure scenario: A report containing raw values such as (p=4.3×10⁻⁹) for one failed-Holm triple and (p=.0098) for another communicates an obvious ranking even if adjectives are prohibited. That cannot and should not be concealed—the numeric record must remain complete—but once A28 establishes a named band, users can construct "top leads" externally.
Required fix: Keep complete numeric reporting for reproducibility, but explicitly state that ordering/ranking by Stage-1 or Stage-2 p-value is an exploratory operation with zero claim licensing. The program itself may not publish "top-N," "strongest," "closest," or threshold-distance rankings. Future selection rules must use a deterministic predeclared rule and carry the selection correction in Findings 1, 8, and 9.
13. MINOR — A28.1; the (p≤0.01) boundary itself does not widen H-KHI-1 if the band is truly claim-free.
Failure scenario: None, provided Stage-1 computation is frozen exactly and the band is never used as evidence. The adjudication threshold remains the frozen H-KHI-1 requirement (p<0.001) on both nulls with Holm correction.
Required fix: Add one sentence:
"Membership in this reporting band has no effect on Stage-2 eligibility, Holm ordering, (m), any p-value, any E-class, or any success/disconfirmation decision."
14. MINOR — A28.2; exhaustive reporting is compatible with G5 and does not itself widen a threshold.
Failure scenario: No defect if Findings 5–7 are adopted. G5 already requires every test run to be logged and prohibits selective reporting.
Required fix: None beyond the standardized presentation and null-calibration requirements above.
15. MINOR — A28.6; the explicit "no intermediate bands / no promising trend" rule is consistent with the program's earlier A1 discipline.
Failure scenario: No defect. It prevents a second discretionary boundary from appearing between screen-positive and adjudicated outcomes.
Required fix: Preserve it, and expand "descriptions" to all program-controlled communications as specified in Finding 6.
16. FATAL — adoption condition; do not freeze A28 as currently written, but it is salvageable.
Failure scenario: As drafted, clause 4 creates the most important back door: a pair selected from roughly 2.5×10⁵ tests can be promoted into a smaller future family and "re-scored from scratch" on the same data. Clauses 1 and 5 additionally make unscoreable/noise-selected triples rhetorically salient without giving the reader the expected null count.
Required fix: A28 may be added only after incorporating Findings 1–12, especially:
   * rename "registered leads" to screen-positive non-adjudicated triples;
   * require Stage-2 scoreability and completion;
   * freeze Stage-1 simulation exactly;
   * report the joint-null expected screen-positive count beside observed N;
   * prohibit individual highlighting and evidential language in all project communications;
   * and make future same-data testing inherit the original selection/multiplicity burden, with independent new evidence required for genuinely fresh confirmation.
Without those changes, do not add A28.
