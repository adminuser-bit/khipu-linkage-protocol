### Audit notes (per resource)

- **CDLI live database — proto-cuneiform holdings (Uruk IV & Uruk III periods)** (5/5) — Best-in-class among audited systems. Live export verified end-to-end (downloaded 1.6MB Uruk III ATF, 5,440 texts). Caveat: intermittent 502s and paginated default (25/page) — must pass limit=; bulk GitHub mirror is stale (see separate row). <https://cdli.earth/search?f[period][]=Uruk III (ca. 3200-3000 BC)>
- **CDLI live database — Proto-Elamite holdings + ATF coverage** (5/5) — ATF coverage for Proto-Elamite is real and near-complete, answering the audit question affirmatively. Sign codes are graphotactic (M-numbers), not phonetic readings. Cross-check: Wikipedia/older literature cites ~1,600 Proto-Elamite tablets; CDLI shows 1,755 entries / 1,597 with ATF — report as range 1,600-1,755. <https://cdli.earth/search?f[period][]=Proto-Elamite (ca. 3100-2900 BC)>
- **HUST-OBC dataset** (5/5) — Undeciphered subset IS explicitly labeled: 62,989 images / 9,411 undeciphered categories vs 77,064 images / 1,588 deciphered categories (paper). GitHub README labels undeciphered sources L, X, Y+H. Structured DB of glyph images, not transliterated running text. <https://github.com/Pengjie-W/HUST-OBC>
- **Open Khipu Repository (OKR)** (5/5) — Directly counted from khipu.db v2.1.0: khipu_main=619 khipus, primary_cord=633, cord=54,403, cord_cluster=14,847, knot=110,677, ascher_cord_color=56,306. This refines the inventory's 'OKR holds 600+ recorded through 2017'. Administered by Open Khipu Research Laboratory (FitzPatrick, Harvard); advisory board Brezine, Ghezzi, Hyland, Medrano, Splitstoser. <https://github.com/khipulab/open-khipu-repository>
- **Voynich EVA transliteration files** (5/5) — Canonical near-complete files per the site: IT (Takahashi), ZL (Zandbergen-Landini), GC (Claston v101); RF designed as comparison reference, not maximum-accuracy reading. Underlying scans free from Yale Beinecke. This is transliteration + scans; no linguistic ground truth exists (system is E-class D/contested). <https://www.voynich.nu/transcr.html>
- **lineara.xyz — Linear A Explorer (R. Hogan, 'mwenge'; GORILA transcriptions via G. ## Method evidence

# Method-Evidence Base: Computational Decipherment, 1990s–2023

Pipeline data for Run 2. Every figure below was verified against the primary source cited (PDF text extraction or direct fetch), not secondary summaries, except where marked "per survey." Problem classes refer to plan v2 §1 shapes (A–E), evaluability gate E0–E3 (§2), and claim ladder L1–L7 (§4). Source disagreements are reported as ranges.

---

## 1. Knight & Yamada 1999 — EM phonetic decipherment (the founding frame)

**Paper:** [A Computational Approach to Deciphering Unknown Scripts](https://aclanthology.org/W99-0906.pdf) (ACL Workshop on Unsupervised Learning in NLP, 1999).

- **Problem class:** unknown script encoding a *known* language — the inverse of shape A. This is "Linear B-shaped": script unknown, language guessable. Never applied to a genuinely unknown system. Gate class of all results: **E1** (known-answer benchmarks only). Ladder level: L5 (phonetic hypotheses), skipping L1–L4 by assumption.
- **Information conditions required:** (i) the underlying language is known and you have its *spoken/phonetic* statistics (sound-triple frequencies); (ii) simplified one-for-one sound-to-character channel; (iii) word separators helpful. Method: noisy-channel + EM over P(character|sound), finite-state transducers.
- **Demonstrated:** Spanish (one page of Don Quixote) 96% phoneme accuracy; Japanese kana (200 sentences) 99%; Chinese 22% syllable accuracy — logographic systems break the method (sparse data over thousands of characters).
- **Successor:** [Knight et al. 2006, Unsupervised Analysis for Decipherment Problems](https://aclanthology.org/P06-2065.pdf) (ACL 2006): EM variants for letter-substitution ciphers (error reduction 68→10 wrong letters with trigrams+smoothing), unknown character-code conversion (Hindi: 25/59 words), phonetic decipherment (Spanish 7% edit distance), and word-based translation-as-decipherment (7/7 Arabic–English country names). Explicitly notes multiple random initializations are used to escape local optima — the seed of the later restart problem.
- **Evaluation design:** ground truth always available by construction (the "unknown" script was manufactured from a known text). No held-out real-world claim anywhere.
- **Press vs demonstrated:** minimal press; the papers themselves invoke Linear B and Mayan as motivation but claim only cipher-style results.

## 2. Snyder, Barzilay & Knight 2010 — Bayesian cognate decipherment (Ugaritic)

**Paper:** [A Statistical Model for Lost Language Decipherment](https://aclanthology.org/P10-1107.pdf) (ACL 2010).

- **Problem class:** lost alphabetic script + *known closely related language* (Hebrew) — corresponds to a shape-B/C situation where the family is already correctly guessed. Result class: **E1** (Ugaritic was deciphered in the 1930s; the answer key existed). Ladder: L5–L6 (sign values + lexical correspondences), with L1–L2 given (signs and word boundaries known).
- **Information conditions required (heavy):** alphabetic writing; word separators present (Ugaritic uses word dividers); a known related language; *inventory and frequencies of Hebrew prefixes/suffixes given*; manual morphological segmentation guidance from a standard Ugaritic grammar; Hebrew Bible as the comparison corpus. Corpus: 7,386 unique Ugaritic word types; cognate evaluation set reported as 2,155 word forms in the paper's own account vs 2,214 pairs in the dataset as reused by Berg-Kirkpatrick & Klein 2011 and Tamburini — **range 2,155–2,214, unresolved**; roughly one-third of the Ugaritic vocabulary has Hebrew cognates.
- **Demonstrated:** 29/30 Ugaritic letters correctly mapped to Hebrew counterparts (96.7% vs 73.3% HMM baseline); cognate translation 60.4% word-type / 66.7% token accuracy; morphemes 75.1% type / 81.3% token. Authors' own caveat: operates purely at vocabulary level, ignores literary context.
- **Evaluation design:** gold cognate dictionary built by annotators from the standard reference dictionary; accuracy against known decipherment. No held-out unknown.
- **Press vs demonstrated:** MIT News headline ["Computer automatically deciphers ancient language"](https://news.mit.edu/2010/ugaritic-barzilay-0630) — omitting that Ugaritic had been deciphered for ~80 years and that the system was *given* the correct related language plus Hebrew morphology. What was shown is method development on a solved instance, i.e., exactly an E1 claim marketed with E3 vocabulary.

## 3. Berg-Kirkpatrick & Klein 2011 / 2013 — combinatorial optimization and the restart problem

**Papers:** [Simple Effective Decipherment via Combinatorial Optimization](https://aclanthology.org/D11-1029.pdf) (EMNLP 2011); [Decipherment with a Million Random Restarts](https://aclanthology.org/D13-1087/) (EMNLP 2013).

- **2011 problem class:** same E1 Ugaritic–Hebrew shape, but with an *easier* task definition: MATCHER assumes the cognate inventories in both languages are known and only learns the matching (objective = minimize summed parameterized edit distance; block coordinate descent, Hungarian algorithm / LP relaxation). Input: just two word lists.
- **2011 demonstrated (verified from PDF):** lexicon accuracy 90.4% vs Snyder et al. 60.4% — *but the paper itself flags the tasks are not comparable* (Snyder reconstructs cognates without knowing the cognate inventory, "which is a harder task"). Alphabet: 28/33 gold character mappings recovered vs Snyder's 29/33 (Ugaritic 30 chars, Hebrew 22, gold matching 33 entries). A clean example of metric- and task-definition-dependent leaderboards in decipherment.
- **2013 (Zodiac ciphers) — the restart-exploitation evidence base:** on Zodiac 408 (homophonic cipher, 408 chars, 54 symbols), EM with a single random restart averages **18% accuracy; 100 restarts average 90%**; harder synthetic Zodiac-340-like ciphers keep gaining past 100K restarts. Also: gold-key initialization gives model score −1467.4 and 92% accuracy, while the *best* local optimum over 1M restarts has better model score (−1466.5) but only 89% accuracy — likelihood and accuracy decouple near the top. Implication logged in plan §9: any decipherment result reported at the best-of-N restarts is conditioned on an evaluation regime that cannot exist for a genuinely unknown script (you can select by objective score, but 2013 shows objective score stops tracking accuracy exactly where it matters).
- **Evaluation design:** known plaintext throughout (E1 by construction).

## 4. Luo, Cao & Barzilay 2019 — NeuroCipher (Ugaritic + Linear B)

**Paper:** [Neural Decipherment via Minimum-Cost Flow: from Ugaritic to Linear B](https://aclanthology.org/P19-1303.pdf) (ACL 2019). Dataset: [github.com/j-luo93/NeuroDecipher](https://github.com/j-luo93/NeuroDecipher), Linear B side built from the Tselentis Linear B lexicon.

- **Problem class:** lost script + known related language, now extended from alphabetic (Ugaritic→Hebrew) to syllabic (Linear B→Greek). Still **E1**: both targets deciphered decades ago. Ladder: L5–L6.
- **Information conditions required:** segmented word lists on both sides; correct known language chosen in advance; for Linear B, a curated cognate lexicon (919 pairs) derived from a modern compilation. Corpus stats (verified, Table 1): Ugaritic 2,214 cognate pairs, 7,353 lost-side / 41,263 known-side tokens, 30/23 symbols; Linear B 919 pairs; Linear B/names variant keeps only 455 Greek proper nouns → ~50% unpaired Linear B words, simulating the historical decipherment-by-toponyms route.
- **Demonstrated (verified from PDF, Table 2/3):** Ugaritic noiseless 93.5% (vs MATCHER 90.4%, +3.1); noisy 65.9% (vs Snyder 60.4%, +5.5) *without* Hebrew morphology supervision; Linear B 84.7%; Linear B/names 67.3%. First automatic cognate results on Linear B.
- **Restart caveat (in the paper itself):** "For Linear B, similar to the finding by (Berg-Kirkpatrick and Klein, 2013), random restarting and choosing the best model based on the objective produces substantial improvements" — selection is by objective, not gold, but Tamburini 2025 reports failing to reproduce the published numbers as typical-run performance (see §8).
- **Press vs demonstrated:** [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2019/07/01/65601/machine-learning-has-been-used-to-automatically-translate-long-lost-languages/): "Machine learning has been used to automatically translate long-lost languages"; system described as "capable of deciphering lost languages," demonstrated on Linear B "the first time this has been done automatically." Actual demonstration: 67.3–84.7% cognate matching on an already-deciphered script with the target language given. The article does carry the key caveat: without knowing the progenitor language (e.g., for Linear A) "the new technique does not work."

## 5. Luo et al. 2021 — undersegmented scripts, phonetic prior, Iberian

**Paper:** [Deciphering Undersegmented Ancient Scripts Using Phonetic Prior](https://aclanthology.org/2021.tacl-1.5.pdf) (TACL 2021).

- **Problem class:** drops two assumptions of §2–4: word segmentation and the identity of the related language. Joint segmentation + cognate ranking, with candidate-language *ranking* as a by-product. Targets: Gothic (E1), Ugaritic (E1), Iberian — a shape-C system (phonetically readable script, opaque language). The Iberian evaluation is the closest thing in this literature to an **E2** design fragment: scoring against externally attested names. Ladder: L2 (segmentation) + L6 (cognate ranking) + a family-ranking claim.
- **Information conditions required:** IPA transcription of the known language; universal phonetic feature embeddings as prior; alphabetic scripts only (stated limitation); corpus sizes: Gothic 40,518 unsegmented tokens; Ugaritic 7,353 (segmented); Iberian 3,466 unsegmented chunks. No word boundaries needed on the lost side.
- **Demonstrated:** Gothic→Proto-Germanic A@10 0.863 (full model), degrading gracefully to more distant relatives (Old Norse 0.597, Old English 0.497); Ugaritic A@1 0.778 vs NeuroCipher 0.659. Iberian: top-10 accuracy on personal names 75% (full model) vs 60% (base), ground truth = Iberian names attested in the Latin *Ascoli Bronze*. Family ranking against Spanish, Arabic, Hungarian, Turkish, Latin, Basque via confidence-coverage plots: "No language seems to have a pronounced advantage over others... accord[s] with the current scholarly understanding that Iberian is a language isolate"; Basque "somewhat stands out," attributed to phonological similarity and minimal numeral overlap, not kinship.
- **Evaluation design:** for solved languages, full ground truth; for Iberian, only the onomastic subset is scored — nothing else about Iberian is claimed. Authors' caveat: reliability of predicted segmentations for true isolates "is beyond our current scope."
- **Press vs demonstrated:** [MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2020/translating-lost-languages-using-machine-learning-1021) "Translating lost languages using machine learning": the algorithm found Basque/Latin "still too different to be considered related" — a *stronger negative claim* than the paper's hedged wording — and states the team's "ultimate goal is for the system to be able to decipher lost languages... using just a few thousand words." Note the survey ([Sommerschield et al. 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.cl-3.5/)) flatly labels Iberian "undeciphered," blurring the shape-C distinction (script readable, language opaque) that the plan's inventory preserves.

## 6. Rao et al. 2009 vs Sproat — the Indus entropy wars

**Core exchange:** [Rao et al. 2009, Entropic Evidence for Linguistic Structure in the Indus Script](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1170391) (Science 324:1165, Brevia); [Sproat 2010, Last Words](https://aclanthology.org/J10-3012.pdf) (Computational Linguistics 36(3)); [Rao et al. 2010 reply](https://aclanthology.org/J10-4016.pdf) (CL 36(4)); [Sproat 2014, A statistical comparison of written language and nonlinguistic symbol systems](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/547119/summary) (Language 90(2)); background: [Farmer, Sproat & Witzel 2004](https://hasp.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/journals/ejvs/article/view/620) (EJVS 11.2).

- **Problem class:** shape D adjudication ("does it encode language?") applied to a shape-A system (Indus). Not decipherment at all — it is the L0 gate question. Current evidential status: **E0/E1**, and Sproat 2014 is the demonstration of *why* (the proposed statistics are non-specific).
- **Information conditions:** Rao et al. used a corpus of ~1,550 lines / ~7,000 sign occurrences derived from Mahadevan 1977 (per their [CL reply](https://aclanthology.org/J10-4016.pdf); the reply does not name the EBUDS extraction explicitly). Comparison sets: Sumerian, Old Tamil, English (words/chars), plus Fortran, DNA, protein sequences, and two *artificial* controls ("Type 1" random, "Type 2" rigid).
- **What Rao et al. actually claimed vs what circulated:** the Science paper concluded conditional entropy of Indus sequences falls in the linguistic band, "increas[ing] the probability" the script represents language; the 2010 reply explicitly retreats to inductive framing: "we do not claim that entropic similarity by itself is sufficient condition for language" and "barring a full decipherment, one cannot prove either the linguistic or nonlinguistic thesis." Press (documented by Sproat 2010): Wired headlined "Artificial Intelligence Cracks Ancient Mystery."
- **Sproat's rebuttals (verified):** (i) a 400-element artificially generated Zipfian text reproduces Rao's Indus conditional-entropy curve — the linguistic "band" is not diagnostic; (ii) the Type 1/Type 2 controls were computationally fabricated straw systems, not real ancient nonlinguistic corpora (real Vinča signs are not distributionally random); a perfectly rigid system "would convey no information whatsoever"; (iii) against Lee et al. 2010 (Pictish): the method misclassifies kudurru deity-symbol sequences as writing and classifies dice-roll sequences as a "syllabic writing system"; (iv) institutional point: "no computational linguists were asked to review these papers."
- **Sproat 2014 (the decisive methodological result for plan §3/§9):** assembled *real* nonlinguistic corpora (Mesopotamian deity symbols, totem poles, Pennsylvania barn stars, weather icons, emoticons) vs 14 language corpora: "all of the non-linguistic symbol systems... behaved the same as the linguistic systems" under the previously proposed measures; his own improved classifier assigns Indus and Pictish to the nonlinguistic side; reliable demonstration of writing "requires supporting empirical evidence, such as a credible decipherment or independent archeological evidence" ([ScienceDaily summary](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/06/140602101559.htm)). Rao et al. and Lee et al. replies appear in the same venue debate ([Cambridge Core record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language/article/on-statistical-measures-and-ancient-writing-systems/D75CB72F35D752E4848CA69AE8E32888)); Rao's standing position is at [his UW page](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~rao/IndusCompLing.html). **Status: unresolved as a Bayesian argument; resolved as a methods verdict — entropy-style statistics detect structured communication, not language (this is the direct ancestor of plan §3's matched-nonlinguistic-null requirement).**

## 7. Corazza et al. 2022 — Sign2Vec_d on Cypro-Minoan

**Paper:** [Unsupervised deep learning supports reclassification of Bronze Age Cypriot writing system](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0269544) (PLoS ONE 17(7):e0269544; Corazza, Tamburini, Valério, Ferrara). Code/vectors/models: [github.com/ashmikuz/sign2vec_d](https://github.com/ashmikuz/sign2vec_d).

- **Problem class:** shape B system (Cypro-Minoan), but the claim is **not decipherment** — it is L1/L3 (sign normalization, structural classification): is Masson's CM1/CM2/CM3 tripartition real, i.e., one script or three?
- **Information conditions:** an annotated sign-image dataset the team built themselves: 2,899 processed sign instances (of 3,499) from 213 inscriptions — the majority of a corpus of ca. 300 inscribed objects, <3,500 sign instances, 96 unique syllabic signs (Olivier's repertory). Split: CM1 1,153 / CM2 1,430 / CM3 316; 1,732 signs on clay tablets. Images vectorized from published drawings; source images remain under publishers' copyright (cited, not redistributed) — a digitization-status fact the plan's data audit should carry.
- **Method:** Sign2Vec_d, a DeepCluster-v2-based unsupervised CNN combining sign shape with positional context (including a sequence-divider prediction task in trigram context).
- **Demonstrated:** embeddings do not separate CM2 as a distinct system; support "a unitary, single Cypro-Minoan script," with shape differences attributable mainly to writing-support material. The Sommerschield survey characterizes the sign-classification performance as roughly two of three signs correct ([survey](https://aclanthology.org/2023.cl-3.5/)). No phonetic values, no language claims.
- **Evaluation design:** intrinsically hard to score — there is no gold standard for the CM1/2/3 question; validation is internal (clustering structure) plus convergence with independent palaeographic arguments (Ferrara). This is the paper's honest limit: an E1/E2-marginal structural claim, testable further only against new finds or independent sign-identity scrutiny.
- **Press vs demonstrated:** no significant inflation observed; the claim stayed inside the paper's scope.

## 8. Tamburini 2023/2025 — coupled simulated annealing and the restart-exploitation critique

**Papers:** [Decipherment of Lost Ancient Scripts as Combinatorial Optimisation Using Coupled Simulated Annealing](https://aclanthology.org/2023.cawl-1.10/) (ACL CAWL 2023); journal version [On automatic decipherment of lost ancient scripts relying on combinatorial optimisation and coupled simulated annealing](https://cris.unibo.it/retrieve/7656690e-fe79-4b54-948b-fabce5723819/frai-8-1581129.pdf) (Frontiers in AI 8:1581129, 2025).

- **Problem class:** same E1 cognate-matching shape as §2–4 (lost script + known relative + cognate list), motivated explicitly by Linear A, Cretan Hieroglyphic, Cypro-Minoan but **not yet applied to any of them**. Ladder: L5–L6 on solved benchmarks.
- **Method:** solutions encoded as k-permutations allowing null/one-to-many/many-to-one sign mappings; single energy function couples sign-mapping quality and lexicon matching (wildcard edit distance + modified linear sum assignment); optimized by Coupled Simulated Annealing with 16 parallel annealers; supports palaeographic constraints (partial known values, wildcards for damaged signs).
- **Benchmarks (2025 version):** Ugaritic/Old Hebrew 2,214 pairs (noiseless) and +2,227 unpaired (noisy); Linear B/Mycenaean Greek 919 pairs; LB/names; Cypriot Syllabary/Arcadocypriot Greek 693 pairs (new); Phoenician/Ugaritic 105 pairs + 58 unpaired; Luwian/Hittite 60 pairs + 75 unpaired.
- **Demonstrated (mean ± std over 4 runs, per Reimers & Gurevych methodology):** U/OH noiseless 95.5±0.83%; LB/MG 89.4±1.81%; LB/names 83.4±2.50%; CS/AG 86.3±1.73%; Ph/Ug 80.5±1.82%; Luv/Hit 47.5±1.67%; U/OH noisy 74.7±1.26% (here the recomputed NeuroCipher 87.6±0.52% *wins* — the CSA advantage is not uniform).
- **The restart-exploitation critique (exact quotes, Frontiers 2025):** on Luo et al. 2019 — "Achieving convergence necessitated restarting the experiments multiple times using various random seeds, a practice impractical in real-world scenarios where a gold standard for comparison is absent"; generally — "the reported Accuracies represent the maximum values obtained after numerous restarts. In real settings we cannot have the gold standard decipherment and restarting the tested method to maximize its performance is not a viable approach." Tamburini's replication of NeuroCipher (with cognancy information removed from input) produced accuracies "slightly lower than reported in the original paper." **Nuance the ledger must keep:** Luo et al. state they selected restarts by *objective score*, not gold labels; but BK13 (§3) shows objective score and accuracy decouple at the top, so best-of-N selection is still epistemically leaky for unknown scripts. Tamburini contrasts his method's convergence without restarts (citing BK13 as the exemplar of restart-dependent practice).
- **Stated limits (2025):** needs segmented, clean corpora ("formidable" to build for a real undeciphered script); needs an extensive cognate list "without any assurance that cognates from the lost language truly exist in the lexicon of the known language"; real unknown systems present "uncertain comparanda." Endorses Sproat (2020): tools assist palaeographers; no autonomous solution. Future aim: application to Aegean scripts — i.e., today the whole CSA line is **E1**.

## 9. Ithaca and predecessors — restoration as calibration (not decipherment)

**Papers:** [Pythia — Restoring ancient text using deep learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.06262) (Assael, Sommerschield, Prag, EMNLP 2019); [Restoring and attributing ancient texts using deep neural networks](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-04448-z) (Assael, Sommerschield et al., Nature 603, 2022).

- **Problem class:** none of the plan's A–E shapes — Ancient Greek is fully readable. Role in this project: **calibration ceiling**. It shows what deep models deliver under *maximal* information conditions (known language, known script, tens of thousands of parallel-genre texts), which bounds expectations as those conditions are removed. Ladder analogue: L7-adjacent output, but on a known language, so no decipherment claim arises.
- **Information conditions:** I.PHI: 78,608 machine-actionable inscriptions after processing, derived from the Packard Humanities Institute corpus (178,551 raw entries per the Nature description); Pythia used the earlier PHI-ML conversion. This is 4–5 orders of magnitude more text than Linear A (~7,400 sign tokens) or Cypro-Minoan (<3,500).
- **Demonstrated:** Pythia: 30.1% character error rate vs 57.3% for human epigraphists; ground truth in top-20 hypotheses 73.5% of cases. Ithaca: restoration 62% alone, CER 26.3%; historians 25% alone → 72% with Ithaca (the human–machine synergy is the headline result); geographic attribution 71% top-1 / 82.1% top-3 (84 regions per the survey record); dating average error 29.3 years, median 3 years (onomastics baseline: 144.4 years). Case study: predictions for the disputed Athenian decrees (e.g., Chalcis decree) favor the "lower" 420s BC dating over the traditional 446/5 BC three-bar-sigma dating — a genuinely checkable engagement with an open scholarly dispute, the closest this literature comes to an E2-style external prediction.
- **Evaluation design (the model for the plan's shadow benchmarks):** artificial damage on held-out real inscriptions (test set = PHI IDs ending in 3 or 4; 1–10 characters hidden); trained human baselines (two graduate epigraphy students); no self-scored claims.
- **Press vs demonstrated:** the paper claims restoration/attribution only, never decipherment. Press slid the verb: Nature's own news headline — ["The AI that deciphers ancient Greek graffiti"](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-022-00701-7); The Decoder — ["New AI deciphers, localizes and dates ancient texts"](https://the-decoder.com/deepmind-new-ai-deciphers-localizes-and-dates-ancient-texts/). "Decipher" in headlines now routinely means "fill lacunae in a known language" — a terminological contamination the project's communications must anticipate.

## Cross-cutting findings

1. **Every quantitative decipherment success 1999–2023 is E1.** All accuracy numbers in §§1–5, 8 were computed against already-deciphered systems (Ugaritic, Linear B, Gothic, Cypriot syllabary, Phoenician, Luwian) or manufactured ciphers. The only E2-flavored fragments: Luo 2021's Iberian names scored against the Ascoli Bronze, and Ithaca's decree-dating engagement.
2. **The information conditions of the successes are exactly what undeciphered targets lack.** The Ugaritic/Linear B pipeline needs: correct related language chosen in advance, segmented word lists, curated cognate inventories (2,214 / 919 pairs), and clean signs. Linear A, Cypro-Minoan, and Indus fail two to four of these each. Tamburini's Luwian/Hittite result (47.5%) shows performance collapsing as conditions degrade even *within* solved pairs.
3. **Evaluation-design pattern (per the survey record):** quantitative scores exist only where ground truth exists; for genuinely undeciphered scripts, published results are structural/clustering measures — entropy bands, sign classification, unitary-script arguments — not decipherment accuracy ([Sommerschield et al. 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.cl-3.5/)).
4. **Restart exploitation is empirically grounded, not hypothetical:** BK13's 18%→90% single-vs-100-restart spread, plus its demonstration that best model score can *exceed* gold-initialization score while accuracy drops (89% vs 92%), plus Tamburini's failed-typical-run replication of NeuroCipher, jointly justify plan §9's rule: methods must report mean±std over runs and must not be tuned by restarting against a known answer.
5. **Press inflation gradient is systematic and directional:** "cognate matching on a solved script" → "deciphers lost languages" (2010 MIT, 2019 Tech Review); "no pronounced advantage for any language" → "too different to be considered related" (2020 MIT on Iberian/Basque); "restores lacunae" → "deciphers" (2022 Ithaca coverage); "entropy consistent with language" → "AI cracks ancient mystery" (2009 Wired, per Sproat). The two communities that avoided inflation: Corazza et al. (structural claim, stayed scoped) and the papers' own limitation sections, which are uniformly more honest than their coverage.
6. **The 2009–2014 Indus exchange is the field's control-group lesson:** Sproat 2014 showed the discriminating statistics fail against *real* nonlinguistic corpora — the direct precedent for plan §3's requirement that is-it-language nulls be structured meaningful nonlinguistic systems, never random or shuffled tokens.

## 1. Oracle Bone sequence (Shape E; the field's only E1→E3 ladder in production)

### 1.1 OBSD — "Deciphering Oracle Bone Language with Diffusion Models" (Guan et al., ACL 2024 Best Paper)
- **Problem class:** Shape E, single-character identification (map undeciphered OBS glyph → modern Chinese character), treated as conditional image generation. Source: [ACL Anthology 2024.acl-long.831](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.831/); [arXiv 2406.00684](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00684).
- **Information conditions:** glyph images only (no context, no transliteration, no sentence-level data); training on deciphered pairs from HUST-OBS + EVOBC — "1,590 distinct characters depicted in 71,698 images" ([ACL PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.831.pdf)).
- **Evaluation design (E1, held-out known characters):** 9:1 train/test split with **test character categories absent from training** ("the genuine challenge of deciphering unseen and novel categories"). Success measured by OCR: a custom ResNet-101 "OBS-OCR" trained on 88,899 modern character categories (self-reported 99.87% accuracy) plus PaddleOCR classify the generated image ([ACL PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.831.pdf)).
- **Demonstrated:** Top-1 41.0% (OBS-OCR) / 30.0% (PaddleOCR); Top-500 64.5% (OBS-OCR). GAN baselines (Pix2Pix, CycleGAN, DRIT++) at 0% Top-1. Ablation: base conditional diffusion alone produced "essentially nonsensical" outputs; the Local Structure Sampling module and zero-shot refinement account for nearly all performance ([ACL PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.831.pdf)).
- **Claimed vs demonstrated:** claims a decipherment *aid* generating "vital clues"; outputs on genuinely undeciphered characters are shown qualitatively only (appendix figures), unverified. **Critical cross-paper fact:** under CipherOBS's stricter unified-1,590-label zero-shot protocol, OBSD scores **1.9% Top-1 / 2.8% Top-10**; under PD-OBS's 200-held-out-class protocol, OBSD scores 18.3/27.5 (HUST-OBC) and 30.4/50.5 (EV-OBC). Reported OBSD zero-shot performance thus spans **1.9%–41% Top-1 across three evaluation designs** — evaluation-protocol sensitivity is first-order, and any Run 3 benchmark must fix the label space and rank metric in preregistration. Sources: [CipherOBS Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09668v1); [PD-OBS Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.10113v1).

### 1.2 PD-OBS — "Interpretable OBS Decipherment through Radical and Pictographic Analysis with LVLMs" (Peng, Zhao, Yu, Fu, Li; arXiv 2508.10113, Aug 2025)
- **Method:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B fine-tuned in stages (radical recognition → pictographic analysis → radical-pictographic mutual analysis), with a **Radical-Pictographic Dual Matching** mechanism replacing direct prediction for zero-shot cases. New dataset: Pictographic Decipherment OBS Dataset, "47,157 Chinese characters annotated with OBS images and pictographic analysis texts" ([arXiv 2508.10113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10113); code PKXX1943/PD-OBS).
- **Evaluation design (E1):** 200 character classes per dataset held out as zero-shot test; remainder 9:1 train/valid. Top-k accuracy + BERT-Score of generated analyses.
- **Demonstrated:** zero-shot Top-10 **53.7%** (HUST-OBC, +26.2 pts over best prior) and **64.1%** (EV-OBC, +13.6); Top-1 zero-shot 16.8/33.3. Validation Top-10 87.8/81.7. BERT-Score of its analyses 79.4–84.9 zero-shot vs GPT-4.1's 67.5–70.9 ([full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.10113v1)).
- **Key negative result for the project:** raw frontier LVLMs are near-useless on this task — **GPT-4.1: 4.3–6.0% Top-1; Qwen-VL-Max: 2.0–4.8%** across settings. Domain fine-tuning + structured matching, not scale, drives all performance ([full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.10113v1)).
- **Claimed vs demonstrated:** "plausible reference results for previously undeciphered oracle characters" — undeciphered outputs are qualitative only.

### 1.3 CipherOBS — "Decoding Ancient Oracle Bone Script via Generative Dictionary Retrieval" (Wu, Zhang, Chen, Xu, Luo, Tang, Xiong; arXiv 2604.09668, Apr 2026)
- **Method:** inverts the pipeline — generate a **synthetic dictionary of plausible OBS variants from every modern character** (evolution-principle-guided generation), then retrieve: ConvNeXt shared encoder, cosine similarity, top-ranked voting. Returns ranked candidates *with exemplar images* for scholar verification ([full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09668v1)).
- **Zero-shot split design (the part our shadow benchmarks should copy):** (i) character-level 90/10 split with **no label overlap** on the 71,698-image / 1,590-character HUST-OBS+EVOBC composite, all baselines re-run under identical preprocessing, unified label space, same OCR judge; (ii) a second, harder OOD test: **14 OBS glyphs discussed only in post-2022 scholarship, with all training corpora and the synthetic dictionary compiled exclusively from pre-2022 sources** — a genuine temporal contamination firewall, the only one in this literature.
- **Demonstrated:** single-pass 21.2% Top-1, **54.3% Top-10, 86.6% Top-50**, 96.9% Top-100; with 20-iteration dictionary refinement 47.5/79.6/98.5/99.9. Direct-retrieval baseline 7.9% Top-1; all generation-paradigm baselines ≤3.4% Top-10. On the 14 OOD glyphs: 100% recovered within Top-100, stratified by character (Qiang best at Top-1; Qi worst) ([full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09668v1)).
- **Claimed vs demonstrated:** explicitly disciplined — "structural similarity to a modern character is a hypothesis-generating step; full decipherment requires broader philological analysis"; the 14 reference interpretations are flagged as "not universally settled decipherments."

### 1.4 AlphaOracle (Liu, Guan, et al.; arXiv 2607.17849, Jul 2026; journal version in The Innovation per Open-Oracle hub)
- **Method:** human-workflow-inspired multi-stage pipeline: (i) rubbing parsing; (ii) radical-based morphological analysis with diachronic modeling; (iii) contextual retrieval with semantic alignment; (iv) philological validation against classical sources — each stage emits "explicit, confidence-weighted evidence chains" culminating in interpretable reports ([arXiv 2607.17849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17849)).
- **Information conditions:** "largest digitized corpus to date" (self-claim); requires images + full-sentence context + digitized classical comparanda. Corpus figure: "approximately 3,000 of the 4,500 OBS characters remain undeciphered" (vs CipherOBS's "~1,500 of ~4,600 decoded" — consistent; keep the dossier's 1,000–1,600 deciphered range).
- **86-expert evaluation:** across multiple test characters, readings "strongly agreed with expert interpretations"; in a study of **86 domain specialists, analysis time reduced 64% and 79% of participants rated it highly useful**. Note: this is a *workflow-efficiency and acceptability* study, not an accuracy benchmark — no held-out accuracy number is reported in the abstract, and the endpoint (expert agreement/time) is weaker than committee-verified decipherment ([arXiv 2607.17849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17849)).
- **Claimed vs demonstrated:** claims resolution of the character "Lao" as toponymic/clan designation — a **candidate** proposal; no evidence in consulted sources that it has passed the Anyang expert-committee mechanism (the field's E3 adjudicator). Treat as E2 candidate pending external verification.

### 1.5 HieroSA (Luo et al., THUNLP-MT; arXiv 2601.05508, Jan 2026)
- **Method:** makes MLLMs stroke-aware: converts logographic/hieroglyphic character bitmaps into "explicit, interpretable line-segment representations in a normalized coordinate space" without handcrafted, language-specific data; positioned as a cross-lingual graphematics tool. Diagnosis motivating it: LLMs treat characters as tokens, MLLMs as pixel grids — both "structurally blind" ([arXiv 2601.05508](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05508)).
- **Demonstrated vs claimed:** demonstrates structure capture on modern + ancient logographs; no decipherment claim. E1 infrastructure. Directly relevant to our L1 (glyph normalization) ladder level.

### 1.6 Ecosystem: Open-Oracle, OBI-Bench, OracleAgent, OracleSage, survey
- **Open-Oracle hub** (maintained by Yuliang Liu, HUST): aggregates HUST-OBC (140,053 images), EVOBC (multi-stage evolution), OBIMD (10,077 OBS images, 93,652 annotated characters — the sentence-context resource), OBI-Bench (5,523 images), PictOBI-20k (20,000 object-character pairs), Oracle-MNIST (30,222), Oracle-P15K (long-tail), Chronicles-OCR; plus OBSD, AlphaOracle, Puzzle Pieces Picker (ICDAR 2024), OracleFusion (ICCV 2025) ([GitHub](https://github.com/Yuliang-Liu/Open-Oracle)).
- **OBI-Bench** (arXiv 2412.01175; ICLR 2025 per repo): 5,523 images, five tasks — recognition, rejoining, classification, retrieval, **deciphering** — over **23 LMMs (6 proprietary, 17 open)**. Finding: LMMs "perform at a level comparable to untrained humans in deciphering tasks" (useful for "creative guesses"/new perspectives) but "far from public-level humans in some fine-grained perception tasks," including GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Qwen-VL-Max ([arXiv 2412.01175](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01175); [GitHub zijianchen98/OBI-Bench](https://github.com/zijianchen98/OBI-Bench)).
- **OracleAgent** (arXiv 2510.26114, Oct 2025): "the first agent system designed for the structured management and retrieval of OBS-related information"; LLM-orchestrated multimodal tools over a knowledge base of **1.4M single-character rubbing images + 80K interpretation texts**; evaluation is retrieval/case-study based (claims to outperform GPT-4o on domain tasks; time-cost reduction). It is a *research-infrastructure* agent, not a decipherment agent ([arXiv 2510.26114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26114)).
- **OracleSage** (arXiv 2411.17837, Nov 2024): LLaVA-based hierarchical visual-semantic + graph reasoning; introduces OracleSem pictographic/semantic annotation dataset ([arXiv 2411.17837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17837)).
- **Survey anchor:** "Oracle bone inscriptions information processing: a comprehensive survey," npj Heritage Science, 2026 ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-026-02511-w)); companion repo [OBI-Future/OBI-Survey](https://github.com/OBI-Future/OBI-Survey).
- **Sequence-level takeaway:** the Oracle Bone program has converged on a shared evaluability design — hold out *known* characters (E1), add temporal firewalls (CipherOBS), report ranked candidates for expert adjudication (E2), with an institutional committee as E3 — exactly the ladder the plan predicts. No 2024–2026 system claims an accepted new decipherment.

## 2. Aeneas (DeepMind; Latin; Shape n/a — fully readable language, method-transfer case)

- **Paper:** "Contextualizing ancient texts with generative neural networks," Nature, 2025 (Assael, Sommerschield, Cooley, Shillingford, Pavlopoulos, Mohamed et al.) ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09292-5); [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/aeneas-transforms-how-historians-connect-the-past/)).
- **Information conditions:** Latin Epigraphic Dataset (LED), **176,000+ inscriptions** curated from EDR, EDH, EDCS-ELT; multimodal (text + images for geographic attribution). This is what a *solved-script, massive-structured-DB* information condition looks like — none of our shape A–D systems approach it.
- **Tasks & solo performance:** restoration incl. **gaps of unknown length** (first model to do so; Top-20 accuracy 73% for gaps ≤10 characters, 58% with unknown length — blog; solo CER 40.5% at known length, "only 15% higher" unknown — Nature summary); geographical attribution 72% top-1 across 62 provinces; dating within ~13 years.
- **"Parallel-generator not oracle," operationally:** (1) the *primary* output is a ranked list of textual/contextual **parallels** retrieved via embedding "historical fingerprints," not an answer; (2) the 23-historian study was staged to separate the value of parallels from the value of predictions — Stage 1 solo baseline, Stage 2 +10 retrieved parallels, Stage 3 +model predictions; (3) measured deltas: parallels rated a useful starting point **75%** of the time, rising to **90%** with predictions; historian restoration CER 39% solo → 33% with parallels → **21%** with parallels+predictions; geographic attribution 27% solo → 68% combined; dating error 31.3 → 14.1 years; confidence +23% (parallels) +21% (predictions); (4) the hybrid beat both the humans alone *and the model alone* on restoration and attribution. Operational translation for our pipeline: the deliverable of any agent is a **candidate-evidence bundle scored by its effect on expert performance**, with the expert study designed to attribute lift to retrieval vs prediction separately ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09292-5)).
- **Transfer limits:** all tasks have ground truth because Latin is read; this is E1-grade validation of *assistance architecture*, not evidence that any component transfers to shape A–D information conditions.

## 3. Otten & Anastasopoulos 2025 — Meroitic (Shape C)

- **Paper:** "Towards Ancient Meroitic Decipherment: A Computational Approach," 2nd Workshop on Ancient Language Processing, 2025, pp. 87–98 ([ACL Anthology 2025.alp-1.11](https://aclanthology.org/2025.alp-1.11/); [PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.alp-1.11.pdf)).
- **Digitization precision:** first Meroitic **machine-readable corpus** — 871 texts/phrases compiled from vocabulary lists (Lobban 2021), example phrases (Rilly 2007; Millet 1968), and three royal narratives (Tañyidamani, Hamadab Stela, Kalabsha); augmented (royal/non-royal name swapping) to 17,257 sentences/phrases, 782,761 words, 1,868 unique word forms; transliterations normalized to Millet's paradigm. Explicitly notes REM's 1,000+ digitized inscriptions remain **untranscribed** for machine use — i.e., the field's structured DB is still scans+images, not text.
- **Evaluation design & results:** Word2Vec embeddings (dims 20–120); intrinsic checks — Late Egyptian control embeddings behave (nswt/Ra/Amun >92% cosine), Meroitic neighbors weak (many below 50–60% cosine); VecMap cross-lingual alignment Meroitic↔Late Egyptian in unsupervised/numeral-seeded/noun-seeded settings: **"None ... were able to do lexicon induction for a dictionary they had not seen before"**; ≤20% accuracy on training dictionaries vs ~70% for a French-English control.
- **Demonstrated vs claimed:** demonstrated = a corpus artifact + a clean *negative* result on embedding-alignment decipherment at this corpus size; claimed = "hopeful" feasibility. For Run 2: this is the honest E1 baseline for Meroitic and evidence that the Melbourne-style "embed and align" family fails at ~10^3-text scale even with a related anchor language.

## 4. arXiv 2608.02999 — SIGIL: the non-specificity construction (shadow-benchmark template)

- **Paper:** "On the Non-Specificity of Statistical Measures Used in Script Decipherment," Nikhil Raghavendra, Aug 2026 ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02999); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02999)).
- **The constructed system (replicate this):** SIGIL is a **deterministic generative emblem system** for a fictional administrative world. Semantics without phonology: a seal "can name a patron deity, attach licensed epithets, identify a guild and a rank within it, list commodity entitlements with tally marks, and close with a validating emblem." Construction mechanics:
  - **Block grammar:** optional opener, dedication, office, ledger, terminal blocks; small probability of adjacent-block inversion.
  - **Finite-pool Pitman-Yor urns** for deities/epithets/guilds/commodities → concentrated head + long tail (Zipf-like without words).
  - **Licensing:** deities license weighted multisets of epithets → above-chance recurring combinations (collocation signatures).
  - **Ligatures:** after the first 1,200 texts, 17 frequent adjacent pairs become registered ligatures later writable as fused signs (palaeographic realism).
  - **Meaningful rare signs:** 20 short-lived administrative issue marks stamped on bounded artifact sets — rarity that is administrative, not phonological.
  - **Regional structure:** four core regions sharing a pantheon with per-region slot conventions + a trading outpost mixing shared deities with two local cults under looser ordering (the dialect-mimic).
  - **Material/directionality:** independent random streams assign 1,100 seal matrices, 1,475 clay sealings, 425 vessel inscriptions; mirror-cut matrices; reading directions 2,972 RTL / 28 LTR.
  - **Released corpus contract:** 3,000 core + 350 outpost texts; 13,717 core tokens; 419 attested of 450 registered signs (+15 outpost-only); 2,753 distinct strings; mean length 4.572, max 12; 51 signs cover 80% of tokens; top sign 6.99%; pinned seeds; immutable sign registry; aligned semantic/physical/spatial records; 27-configuration common-seed sensitivity grid. Size and inventory deliberately matched to the Indus studies.
- **Evaluation protocol (replicate this too):** a **54-method literature registry closed before scoring**; entries classed as 6 exact-scored / 18 reconstructions / 14 descriptive / 10 blocked / 6 excluded. Exact scoring requires (a) independent reproduction of the published Indus outcome and (b) a source-defined categorical decision rule — "continuous resemblance is never scored." Controls: rigid repeated template, equiprobable max-entropy, i.i.d. Zipf-Mandelbrot, within-text shuffles, pinned Brown-corpus word and character sequences.
- **Results:** SIGIL matches the Indus outcome on **all six exact-scored criteria**: adjacent/total repetition ratio 0.646 vs Indus 0.575 (both "non-linguistic" under the source's own rule); terminal Gini asymmetry ΔG=0.0920 RTL p=0.001 (Indus 0.1788, p=0.001); terminal entropy asymmetry ΔS=−0.2920 RTL p=0.001 (Indus −0.4144); three LNRE model rejections at matching orders. Descriptive battery reproduces the Indus-like profile throughout: Zipf-Mandelbrot b=2.574 vs published EBUDS 2.59; conditional entropy 3.609 nats in the "linguistic band" between Brown words (3.917) and characters (2.421); Witten-Bell perplexity curve shape matches published Indus values; positional asymmetry 39 initial vs 10 final signs for 80% coverage (Indus: 82 vs 23); masked-sign restoration 84.88% within 90%-posterior candidate set (published Indus figure 74%); transition-graph reciprocity/connectance below shuffle nulls exactly as Indus; **Lee decision rule assigns SIGIL "writing: syllables" in all 27 sensitivity configurations**; outpost-vs-core likelihood separation reproduces the Rao et al. West Asia "dialect" signature from regional emblem practice alone.
- **Sequential decipherment stress test (kills dictionary-coverage arguments):** a source-constrained reconstruction of the Yajnadevam-style cryptanalytic protocol applied to SIGIL reaches in-sample dictionary coverage of **83.0% (English), 88.13% (Sanskrit), 67.73% (Tamil)** — three mutually incompatible "readings" of the same meaningless-phonologically corpus; grouped held-out coverage falls to 74.23/61.01/44.05 vs random-key means 6.50/5.77/9.48; Sanskrit script+adaptive protocols reach 97%+ in-sample but random script keys already average 27.87%; six bootstrap keys each parse >93.5% of the corpus yet **agree on only 9.30% of sign values** (below 1-in-49 chance for rare signs). High coverage coexists with no identification.
- **For our shadow benchmarks:** the paper's own limitation is explicit — "the construction does not decide what the Indus signs encode." Our paired Indus-shaped worlds must inherit: pre-registered method registry with reproduce-then-score gating; categorical-rule-only scoring; meaningful (not shuffled) nonlinguistic null with slot grammar + urn lexicon + licensing + regional variation; grouped held-out + key-stability diagnostics for any "reading" claim; pinned seeds and a public corpus contract.

## 5. Khipu record linkage (Shape D; the only E2 with colonial ground truth)

## 8. Cross-cutting findings for Run 2

1. **Evaluation-protocol sensitivity dwarfs method differences** (OBSD: 1.9%→41% Top-1 across three protocols). Preregistration must pin label space, rank cutoffs, judge (OCR vs human), and split construction.
2. **Raw frontier models fail at glyph-level work everywhere measured** (GPT-4.1 ≤6% Top-1 OBS; LMMs ≈ untrained humans on OBI-Bench deciphering; LVLM token descriptions unusable for complex glyphs). Every success story is a *narrow fine-tuned system with structured inductive bias* (radicals, dictionaries, evidence chains) producing **ranked candidates for expert adjudication**.
3. **The candidate-generator + expert-lift evaluation pattern** (Aeneas 23-historian staged study; AlphaOracle 86-specialist study) is now the field's de facto E2 instrument; its endpoints (time saved, confidence, CER-with-assistance) are measurable but are *not* decipherment accuracy — keep the claim types separate in the ledger.
4. **Temporal firewalls exist in the wild** (CipherOBS pre-2022 training / post-2022 test) and should be mandatory in all our designs.
5. **SIGIL supplies a complete, public recipe** for the meaningful-nonlinguistic null — slot grammar, Pitman-Yor lexica, licensing, ligatures, administrative rare signs, regional outposts, direction conventions, pinned-seed corpus contracts, pre-closed method registries, categorical-rule-only scoring, and key-stability diagnostics. Our Indus-shaped paired worlds should be built to its spec and red-teamed against it.

### Maya script — undeciphered sign residue

- **Region / period:** Mesoamerica: Mexico (Yucatán, Campeche, Chiapas), Guatemala, Belize, Honduras, El Salvador; ~491 sites documented (Bonn TWKM) · Classic core c. 250-900 CE; three surviving codices (Dresden, Madrid, Paris) later; earliest antecedents not re-verified here
- **Shape:** E · **Corpus:** Texts: ~8,000 documented hieroglyphic texts, ~400,000 glyph blocks, ~2.4M estimated sign tokens (Bonn ClassicMayan portal) vs '~5,000 surviving text carriers' (SLUB Dresden) — range preserved. Sign inventory: ~650 distinct characters incl. ~200 syllabograms (SLUB); Unicode preliminary list 391 signs = 150 syllabograms + 241 logograms (Vail 2023, L2/23020, building on Macri & Looper 2003). Residue: '10 to 15% of the symbols are not known' (EPFL/Idiap MAAYA) ≈ SLUB's 'about 90% deciphered more or less reliably'; separately, >90% of TEXTS readable with reasonable accuracy as of 2020 (Wikipedia) and ~85% of text carriers readable (SLUB) — sign-level vs text-level percentages must not be conflated. Dresden Codex: 250 of 350 distinct characters deciphered (SLUB).
- **Digitization:** Strong, multiple structured DBs: Maya Hieroglyphic Database (MHD; founded Macri, directed Looper, CSU Chico; NSF/NEH; mayadatabase.org) with mayacorpus.org front end exposing 200,000+ glyph blocks searchable by site/object/transliteration/T-number — structured transliteration DB, not just scans; Text Database and Dictionary of Classic Mayan (Univ. Bonn, classicmayan.org) — ~8,000 text carriers catalogued, sign catalog, open access; Maya Codices Database (mayacodices.org) for the codices; Unicode encoding in progress via Script Encoding Initiative (L2/23020, 2023).
- **Notes:** NEW ENTRY patching flagged gap. Exactly parallel to the Oracle Bone undeciphered-character-subset entry: script structure and language family known (Ch'olan-Tzeltalan readings; script logosyllabic), problem is the residue of rare/unread signs and damaged texts. Computational work exists but is recognition/corpus-building, not decipherment: MAAYA project (Idiap/EPFL Digital Humanities Lab/Bonn/Geneva) digitized glyphs of the three codices for automated identification and annotation DB; CNN glyph classification (Can, Odobez & Gatica-Perez, ACM JOCCH 2018; shape representations JOCCH 2016). No accepted machine decipherments of residue signs. Percentages differ by unit (signs vs texts vs text carriers); all consulted figures fall in the 85-90% solved band, i.e. ~10-15% residue, matching the critic's estimate.

### Naxi Dongba and Geba scripts (with Ersu Shaba note)

- **Region / period:** East Asia: Lijiang region, Yunnan, China (Naxi people); manuscripts dispersed — ~10,000 in US/UK/France/Germany libraries, ~15,000 in Asian institutions (Sci Data 2025) · Script invention traditionally referenced to Song dynasty (early 13th c.) in manuscript tradition; surviving manuscripts mainly 19th-20th c. (Library of Congress collection); living ritual use continues
- **Shape:** D · **Corpus:** Dongba manuscripts: ~20,000 volumes (Wikipedia, Dongba Manuscripts) to ~25,000 (Nature Sci Data 2025) — range preserved; >1,000 different scriptures (Sci Data 2025). Geba: 'few texts' known, used mainly to transcribe mantras and gloss dongba pictographs. Ersu Shaba: ~200 glyphs (Wikipedia).
- **Digitization:** Library of Congress Naxi Manuscript Collection digitized selections online; Harvard Naxi collection catalogued (Li & Peters 2024 provenance study); Dongba1800 open dataset for character DETECTION: 1,800 annotated page images, 111,702 character instances (Sci Data 2025) — image dataset, not a transliteration corpus; Unicode not finalized (Richard S. Cook research notes toward Naxi encoding; Geba ISO 15924 code Nkgb).
- **Notes:** SCOPE RULING (answering the critic's question directly): Dongba is NOT an undeciphered script — it is a living, academically documented tradition (UNESCO Memory of the World 2003; ~60 practicing dongba priests can read/write it per Omniglot; taught in local revival programs). It belongs in this inventory only as a shape-D 'reading = reciting' semasiographic comparandum parallel to the Kuna/Wiigwaasabak entries: pictographs are mnemonic, with no fixed word order, texts 'may be intelligible only to its author' (Wikipedia), so the reading problem is documentation/endangerment, not decipherment. The genuinely under-documented member is GEBA: syllabary with symbols lacking fixed phonetic values, few texts, 'little understood' (Omniglot) — a small D/E-adjacent documentation debt, still not a classic decipherment target since the language (Naxi) is alive. Ersu Shaba (Ersu people, Sichuan): ~200-glyph semasiographic system where ink color is meaning-bearing (black vs white star-glyph = 'dim' vs 'shining'); only Shaba priests literate; documented by Sun Hongkai (2009); same D-class ruling. ### Tărtăria tablets

- **Region / period:** Tărtăria, Săliștea commune, Transylvania, Romania (~30 km from Alba Iulia) · Radiocarbon on associated finds suggests c. 5300 BCE; tablets themselves baked after discovery so not directly datable; stratigraphic attribution contested (some argue a later layer)
- **Shape:** D · **Corpus:** 3 clay tablets (2 rectangular, 1 round of ~6 cm), found 1961 by Nicolae Vlassa together with 26 figurines, a shell bracelet, and skeletal remains; sign counts per tablet not stated in consulted sources — do not import unsourced figures
- **Digitization:** No dedicated structured corpus; photographs widely reproduced in literature; the signs are subsumed in Merlini's DatDas Danube-signs databank (see existing Vinča entry)
- **Notes:** NEW ENTRY splitting the Old European long tail out of the Vinča entry. Triple dispute: (1) dating — associated-material C14 of ~5300 BCE would predate Mesopotamian writing, but Vlassa never documented the excavation circumstances or stratigraphy; (2) authenticity — forgery allegations center on similarity of some signs to Sumerian signs reproduced in publications available in Romania at the time; (3) writing status — supporters of a writing/proto-writing reading include Gimbutas, Merlini, Haarmann, Lazarovici; skeptics include Renfrew ('simple pictographs') and Milisauskas. No decipherment claim has any standing since sign-hood itself is unresolved. Keep D.

### Gradeshnitsa tablets

- **Region / period:** Gradeshnitsa village, Vratsa Province, northwestern Bulgaria; held at Vratsa Archeological Museum · 4th millennium BC (per Wikipedia; consulted source gives no finer resolution — note other literature is often cited with 5th-millennium dates, unverified here)
- **Shape:** D · **Corpus:** Clay tablet(s) with incised marks unearthed 1969; exact tablet and sign counts not specified in consulted sources — flag as verification debt
- **Digitization:** None found; no digital corpus or online museum record identified in consulted sources
- **Notes:** NEW ENTRY splitting the Old European long tail out of the Vinča entry. Reception per Steven Fischer (quoted in Wikipedia): 'the current opinion is that these earliest Balkan symbols appear to comprise a decorative or emblematic inventory with no immediate relation to articulate speech' — i.e., neither logographic nor phonographic. Bulgarian popular/nationalist claims of 'oldest writing' circulate but have no peer-reviewed decipherment behind them. Keep D; low priority; primary documentation debt (counts, dating) noted.

### Karanovo seal (stamp seal)

- **Region / period:** Karanovo tell, Maritsa Valley near Nova Zagora, Bulgaria · '~6,800 years old' per Virtual Museum of the Inscriptions (i.e. c. 4800 BCE); no independent verification of dating obtained in this pass
- **Shape:** D · **Corpus:** Single round clay stamp seal: 6 cm diameter, 2 cm thick, 2 cm handle; incised signs divided into four quadrants by the arms of a cross; found in a burnt (fire-destroyed) house; excavated by Georgi I. Georgiev
- **Digitization:** None; photographs/drawings in literature and the prehistory.it 'Virtual Museum of the Inscriptions' page only
- **Notes:** NEW ENTRY splitting the Old European long tail out of the Vinča entry. Signs described as 'straight, abstract and... impossible to connect... to any forms belonging to the real world' (prehistory.it). Richard Flavin's proposal that the signs resemble western zodiac constellations in sequence is a contested hypothesis, not consensus. As a unicum stamp seal it is closer to the Anau-seal problem class than to a text corpus; whether the marks are writing at all is unresolved. Keep D; effectively undecipherable in isolation.

### Kassite language

- **Region / period:** Mesopotamia: Babylonia (Kassite dynasty of Babylon); names also in Nuzi and other Akkadian archives · Attested c. 18th-7th century BCE; Kassite dynasty ruled Babylon 16th-12th century BCE
- **Shape:** C · **Corpus:** No connected texts in Kassite whatsoever. Attestation: one Kassite-Babylonian vocabulary of 48 entries (god names, nouns, verbs, adjectives); a list of 19 Kassite personal names with Akkadian equivalents (neo-Assyrian); scattered items in Akkadian lexical texts and the 'Babylonian Pharmacopoeia'; numerous proper names (people, deities, places, horses/equids) in Akkadian documents c. 1360-850 BCE; horse/chariot technical terminology. Size of lexicon disagrees by source — 'only a few dozen words are known' (Wikipedia) vs 'a couple hundred words of Kassite origin in late Akkadian, some 10% divine names' (Encyclopaedia Iranica) — range preserved.
- **Digitization:** No dedicated Kassite corpus or database; all attestation is embedded in standard Akkadian cuneiform text editions and lexical-list publications (script fully readable)
- **Notes:** NEW ENTRY patching flagged gap. Textbook C-shape: perfectly readable cuneiform, opaque language — like Hattic but strictly worse-attested (Hattic at least has passages and bilinguals; Kassite has none). Grammar reconstruction impossible on current evidence. Classification: unclassified; not Semitic; relation to Elamite doubtful; proposed Hurro-Urartian ties (Fournet 2011, building on lexical comparisons) remain unsettled — could be a distinct Hurro-Urartian language, a southern Hurrian dialect, or unrelated. Small Indo-Iranian contact stratum (e.g. šuriias 'sun god'; Abi-rattaš with chariot term) is significant historically but does not classify the language. Key literature: Balkan 1954 Kassitenstudien; Pinches 1907/1917. Only new-text discovery (a Kassite bilingual or archive) would change status; no computational angle exists.

### Pre/Early Dynastic Egyptian potmarks and Abydos tomb U-j tags

- **Region / period:** Egypt: Abydos Umm el-Qa'ab (tomb U-j); potmark corpora from Tarkhan, Adaïma, Minshat Abu Omar, Kafr Hassan Dawood, Tell el-Farkha, and other sites · Tomb U-j tags: Naqada III, c. 3320-3150 BCE (contextual + radiocarbon ~3200 BCE); potmarks: Predynastic through Early Dynastic (database coverage extends to Middle Kingdom)
- **Shape:** D · **Corpus:** U-j: ~150 bone/ivory labels (The Past) from a single tomb, each c. 2 x 1.5 cm bearing 1-4 glyphs; 43 carry only numeral strokes; associated inscribed pottery and clay seal impressions (impressions dated as early as c. 3400 BCE per Archaeology Magazine 1999). Potmarks: >3,360 individual Early Dynastic potmarks in the International Potmark Workshop database; site corpora published separately (Tarkhan, Adaïma, Kafr Hassan Dawood).
- **Digitization:** Potmarks: database-driven website potmark-egypt.com (International Potmark Workshop, est. 2005; van den Brink a principal contributor), with sign catalog and bibliography — structured but specialist-run; U-j tags: print publication Dreyer, Hartung & Pumpenmeier 1998, Umm el-Qaab I: Das prädynastische Königsgrab U-j und seine frühen Schriftzeugnisse; no open structured U-j tag database found
- **Notes:** NEW ENTRY patching flagged gap; shape D, directly parallel to the Chinese Neolithic signs entry. The dispute is not 'can we decipher it' but 'is it (already) writing': Dreyer's rebus/phonetic readings of tag signs as quantities and localities are influential, and Met curator Jim Allen described the system as rebus-based with 'approximately 70 percent of these predynastic hieroglyphs... translated' (Archaeology Magazine 1999) — but that figure is a 1999 popular-press claim to treat cautiously, and skeptical positions on the phonetic readings and on the 3200 BCE priority-over-Sumer framing persist (Egypt-vs-Mesopotamia priority explicitly flagged as unresolved in the same source). Ordinary potmarks are generally treated as a marking system rather than writing. Continuity with mature hieroglyphic makes this a calibration case for the origin-of-writing boundary, like proto-cuneiform.

### Sicel (Siculian)

- **Region / period:** Eastern Sicily, Italy (key find-spot Montagna di Marzo) · Late 6th to 4th century BCE
- **Shape:** C · **Corpus:** Fewer than 30 inscriptions total plus ~25 glosses in ancient authors; per Markus Hartmann only six inscriptions are even partly intelligible; key text: amphora from Montagna di Marzo (late 6th-early 5th c. BCE)
- **Digitization:** No dedicated database found; Sicilian epigraphy is being re-edited digitally via ERC Crossreads / I.Sicily (Oxford) — coverage of Sicel material not verified item-level
- **Notes:** NEW ENTRY (boundary pass — QUALIFIES). Script is Greek-derived (native alphabet on Western Greek, probably Euboic-Chalkidic model) and readable; the language is the problem: Indo-European, dominant view Italic (forms like geped, dedaxed, Kup(a)ra), but phonology diverges from expected Italic sound changes and 'it is difficult to determine much about this language beyond that it was Indo-European.' Opaque through corpus brevity and interpretation difficulty, exactly like the neighboring Elymian entry already in the inventory. Keep C.

### Sicanian

- **Region / period:** Central-western Sicily, Italy (Sicani territory) · Classical period attestation; whether the language survived as distinct into classical times is itself questioned
- **Shape:** C · **Corpus:** 'A few short inscriptions using the Greek alphabet' — no exact count in consulted sources; plus toponymic evidence (recurrent suffixes -ina, -ana, -ara, -ssus/-ssa)
- **Digitization:** None found; no corpus edition or database identified in this pass
- **Notes:** NEW ENTRY (boundary pass — QUALIFIES, weakest corpus of the three Sicilian cases). Greek alphabet, readable; 'except for names, they have not been translated.' Language unclassified due to lack of data; proposed as a possibly non-Indo-European substrate with suggested but uncertain Tyrsenian connections. Scholars note toponyms may be better evidence than the inscriptions. Keep C with the caveat that the corpus may be too small ever to test any classification — closer to Sitovo-class than Elymian-class evidentially.

### Messapic

- **Region / period:** Salento / Apulia, southeastern Italy · Mid-6th century BCE to at least 2nd century BCE; extinct after Roman conquest
- **Shape:** C · **Corpus:** ~600 inscriptions; 36% of the corpus consists solely of personal names from burials; longer texts exist at Grotta della Poesia (Roca Vecchia) but 'have not been fully exploited by scholars yet'
- **Digitization:** Print corpus: Monumenta Linguae Messapicae (MLM, 2002, de Simone & Marchesini); no open structured database identified in this pass
- **Notes:** NEW ENTRY (boundary pass — INCLUDED, with explicit boundary flag). Alphabet Greek-derived (Laconian-Tarantinian model), fully readable; language securely Indo-European, Paleo-Balkan, with debated Illyrian ties and a proposed 'Illyric' grouping with Albanian. Included because 'only a few inscriptions have been definitely deciphered' — i.e., most of a 600-item corpus remains untranslatable, matching the Phrygian precedent already in the inventory as a C-case. BOUNDARY FLAG: this is the best-understood system added in this patch; if the inventory later tightens C-criteria to exclude classified-but-fragmentary IE languages, Messapic (and Phrygian) exit together — they should be treated consistently.
